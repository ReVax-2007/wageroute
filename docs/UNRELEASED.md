### Batch 22 — the reserve lifecycle remembers how it was written

- Schema 9 adds `bill_reserves.strategy` (the funding strategy in force when the cheque wrote the claim), `bill_reserves.due_day` (the due day it was written against) and `bill_reserves.settled_by` (the transaction that closed it), with a partial index on the link. The migration backfills each live row from its bill; no amount, state or date changes, and a pre-v9 database upgrades on first launch.
- The plan now receives the month's open claims (`monthClaims`) instead of a list of reserved bill ids. Every claim reduces the month's need, and a spread cheque tops the month up to its normal share. Switching a carried bill to every-check no longer reserves the month twice, switching a spread bill to one check reserves only the remainder, and a third switch reserves nothing more.
- A payment in a later month settles an earlier claim when that claim's funding cheque fell **after** the bill's due day in its own cycle — the real carry rule. It is no longer limited to `last_check`: a monthly `first_check` cheque on 31 January funds a 1 February due date. A payment dated before the new month's cheque settles the carry, not the new claim, because that cheque had not been received yet.
- Deleting or moving a payment re-opens exactly the claim `settled_by` names, then falls back to the old cycle lookup for rows paid before v9.
- A restored reserve row takes its bill's strategy and due day when the file states neither, and starts with no `settled_by` link. The example backup and the backup allowlist carry the columns.
- `src/main/db/reserves.test.ts` gains five lifecycle cases (carry under `first_check`, payment-before-cheque ordering, delete re-opening the carry, and both strategy switches), `migrations.test.ts` gains the v9 case, and `backup-merge.test.ts` proves a restored claim takes its bill's strategy unless the file states one.

### Batch 23 — settle, reopen and display the same claim

- The commit backstop now skips a payment already named by `settled_by`, so one payment can no longer close two months of carry. `reopenCycleClaim` touches only unlinked rows, so deleting or moving one payment leaves a claim another live payment closed paid.
- Every claim reduces the month's remaining need, whatever strategy wrote it. `billFundingReserveCents` tops a spread bill up to its normal share when a claim exists (smoothing a partly paid bill across the cheques still to come), and `recordBillReserves` caps a month's open claims at the bill's monthly equivalent.
- Each claim stores its own `due_day`; a later edit to the bill cannot change whether an old claim carries.
- The Priority Wall passes this month's cash payments and open claims into the next-check projection, and reports reserved and paid claims as separate sums instead of counting paid money as reserved.
- The Allocator preview subtracts `creditCents` from `paidCents`, matching `commitPaycheck` for a bill charged to a card.
- Restore refuses a nameless category or one longer than 60 characters instead of writing a row the app cannot save, and relinks an entry that names its category only by spelling.
- New cases: four in `reserves.test.ts` (one payment one carry, delete leaves another payment's claim paid, third switch reserves nothing, due-day edit), four in `backup-merge.test.ts` (stated due day, nameless category, long name, text-only relink), and the v9 migration asserts the due-day backfill. `npm test` is **324 tests across thirty-eight files**.

### Batch 24 — pay only what the money covers

- A payment now closes only the claims its own amount fully covers, oldest first. Same-cycle settlement used to mark every open instalment `paid`, so a single 60,000 payment closed 120,000 of claims. A claim is never part-paid: a payment smaller than the next claim leaves it open.
- `recordBillReserves` caps a month on its **open** claims. A claim closed by a card charge no longer blocks the cash reserve the plan asks for, so the allocator and the writer agree again.
- After a payment lands mid-month, `billFundingReserveCents` divides the remaining need across the guaranteed cheques still to come. A 120,000 bill with 60,000 paid before the last semimonthly cheque now withholds 60,000, not 30,000. (Batch 26 counts the real anchored series instead of the guaranteed slots; see below.)
- A pre-v9 payment with no `settled_by` link re-opens the earlier carried claim it closed, not only a row in the payment's own month.
- New cases: four in `reserves.test.ts` (an instalment-sized payment, a short payment, a card-closed claim, a legacy carry re-open), two in `cadence.test.ts` (mid-month and monthly recovery), the v9 migration's due day is now 28 so the backfill is actually exercised, and the restored-reserve case inherits a due day of 30. `npm test` is **330 tests across thirty-eight files**.

### Batch 25 — the wall says which money is still open

- A month with one paid instalment and one open claim used to print **Paid this month**, because any paid row won the chip. It now reads **Reserved** while anything is open and **Paid** only when every claim for the month is closed; the summary above the table already reported the two sums separately.
- The Priority Wall and the dashboard passed their own `<tbody>` to `DataTable`, which supplies one, so both pages contained a nested body and React reported a hydration error. They now pass rows only.
- New `PriorityWall.test.ts` mounts the wall in jsdom: the mixed month reads **Reserved**, a closed month reads **Paid**, the summary prints `$600.00 reserved this month · $600.00 paid`, the next check keeps only the $300 a cash payment and a claim leave owed, and the table has exactly one `<tbody>`. `npm test` is **333 tests across thirty-nine files**; lint 127 files.

### Batch 26 — derive claim states from the payments

- Claim states are now **derived** by `reconcileBillReserve`, which replays every live bill-linked transaction for the bill in date order. The incremental settle/reopen functions (`settleBillReserveForPayment`, `settleEarlierBillReserve`, `settleEarlierBillReserves`, `reopenBillReservesSettledBy`, `reopenCycleClaim`) are gone.
- **A bill paid in instalments settles.** Two 60,000 payments now close a 120,000 claim; the old matcher broke on the first claim larger than one payment, so the claim stayed `reserved` forever and the wall printed reserved and paid money for the same bill. `settled_by` names the payment that completed it.
- **A refund is not a payment.** A positive bill-linked row takes coverage back from the most recent contribution instead of closing a claim, so a refunded month reads `reserved` again.
- **Deleting or moving one payment cannot disturb a claim another payment paid.** Previously a linked v9 delete also ran the legacy cycle fallback, which re-opened an unrelated unlinked claim. Replaying the remaining payments leaves the other claim paid and renames the payment that covers it.
- **An empty claim no longer blocks the queue.** The carry loop stopped at the first zero-cent claim, which kept every eligible claim behind it reserved; the replay skips non-positive claims.
- **The commit backstop replays every bill** (`reconcileAllBillReserves`), including a merged backup, and `mergeBackup` runs it once at the end, so a partial restore's claims and payments agree without a manual edit.
- **A fifth weekly or third biweekly cheque counts.** `remainingChecksInMonth` used the guaranteed slot count and treated a real later cheque in the same month as if it did not exist, concentrating a part-payment's remainder on one cheque and risking an all-or-nothing shortfall. With a payday anchor it now counts the actual series; without one, the guaranteed fallback is unchanged.
- The month's cap still counts **open** claims only. That is deliberate: a claim closed by a card charge released its earmark but did not take cash out of the pool, so the later cheques still have to cover the bill's cash obligation. The card case in `reserves.test.ts` pins it.
- New cases: instalments closing one claim, the legacy unlinked claim surviving a linked delete, a refund re-opening its claim, a zero claim skipped, and anchored weekly/biweekly remaining-cheque counts. `npm test` is **339 tests across thirty-nine files**; lint 127 files.

### Batch 27 — the wall shows only money still owed

- **One annual or quarterly payment clears every slice it covers.** A 1,200,000 annual premium gathered twelve 100,000 claims from twelve cheques but the old carry rule required the cheque's day of month to fall after the bill's due day, so the single payment closed one slice and left eleven reading `reserved`. Smoothed bills now walk every earlier claim whose cheque had arrived, whatever the due day.
- **The wall says what the next check still owes.** A bill already covered this month shows $0.00 and **Nothing more due** instead of naming a check that has nothing left to do; a paused bill shows $0.00 and **Paused** instead of a reserve it will never take.
- **The table fits its card.** Seven columns of nowrap money plus a long funding hint forced a horizontal scroll and pushed the numbers out of the card. Amount, cadence and due day now sit under the bill name, leaving four columns, and only the numeric cells refuse to wrap.
- **The allocator's breakdown hides settled lines.** Bills whose reserve is zero are left out, with one line saying how many are already covered this month, so a paid wall no longer looks like an open question.
- **A bill-linked adjustment is not a payment.** `listBillPaymentsThisMonth` now reads only `bill_payment` and `expense` rows, and accepts an as-of day so a future-dated entry does not release a month before it happens. `commitPaycheck` passes the cheque's own date.
- **A refund cannot grow the wall.** The renderer's paid-per-bill map clamps at zero, matching the repository, so a net-positive bill no longer increases what is withheld.
- **Deleted parents stop counting.** `claimsThisMonthFor` and `recordBillReserves` join live bills and cheques, the wall filters claims to live bills, and deleting an account now replays the claims its payments used to hold open.
- New cases: an annual payment clearing every slice, a bill-linked adjustment ignored, the as-of ceiling, and deleting the account re-opening the bill, plus three jsdom cases for the zero, covered and paused rows. `npm test` is **345 tests across thirty-nine files**; lint 127 files.

### Batch 28 — inputs, cards, buffers and goal pace

- **Every money field is a money field.** Bill, entry, goal, account, paycheck-card and adjustment amounts now use `MoneyInput`, which keeps digits, the first decimal point and at most two decimals, and shows an on-screen keypad on phones (Batch 40 later let typed commas survive and settle on blur). Percent fields use `PercentInput`; the allocator's income field and the spreadsheet's inline amount editor share the same sanitiser. An entry can still be negative, but the minus sign is only accepted at the start.
- **An adjustment carries a reason.** The dialog offers mistake, rounding, interest earned, interest charged, investment gain and investment loss, filtered to the account kind. The reason chooses the memo and category (`Interest earned` → Income, gain/loss → Investing), and a gain that lowers the balance or a loss that raises it is refused rather than mislabelled.
- **A card can be adjusted by what is owed, what is available, or how full it is.** The adjust dialog on a credit account offers three modes: balance owed, available credit, and utilisation percent. Each resolves to a target owed amount and previews the balance, available credit and utilisation that will be saved; available credit and percent are clamped at zero.
- **The emergency fund can carry a manual buffer.** Auto mode adds an optional extra cushion (`emergencyBufferCents`) on top of months × essentials, for one-offs the monthly average misses. Blank, negative and non-finite values add nothing, and zero essentials still honours the buffer.
- **One-time income has its own section.** The dashboard lists this month's income entries that are not paychecks (`transactions.paycheck_id IS NULL`), with a total and an **Add income** dialog. The money is written to the ledger and deliberately stays out of paycheck routing.
- **A goal can be paced or paused.** Schema 10 adds `goals.funding_pace`: `asap` funds the remaining need on the next cheque, `by_date` caps each cheque at `ceil(need / checksLeft)` so the goal still arrives by its target date, and `paused` removes it from the allocator entirely. The goal dialog sets the pace next to priority; the goal card has a pause/resume button and chips for **Funding paused** and **Paced to <date>**. Existing goals default to `asap`, so an upgrade writes the same allocations it did before.
- New cases: five sanitiser cases in `money.test.ts`, four buffer cases in `emergency.test.ts`, three allocator cases in `domain.test.ts` (paused goal, paced goal, paced goal with no target date), four adjustment-reason cases in `adjustments.test.ts`, and the v10 migration case (existing goal defaults to `asap`, the CHECK refuses an unknown pace). `npm test` is **362 tests across forty-two files**; lint 132 files.

### Batch 30 — the ledger can live in a folder you choose

- **Settings → Where your data lives** shows the current folder and can point WageRoute at a NAS share, external disk or synced folder. The choice is recorded in `storage.json` in the per-user folder, outside the database.
- Picking a folder returns what it holds: **open** an existing ledger (the local one is untouched), **move** the current ledger into an empty folder (with a `pre-move-*.db` safety copy), or **start empty** there. **Move it back to this computer** copies the external ledger over the local one after a `pre-return-*.db` copy.
- Every switch validates the folder, refuses a ledger newer than this build, writes the pointer, closes the database and restarts. Boot opens the chosen file through the existing path.
- `wageroute.lock` records hostname, PID and start time and is refreshed every 30 seconds. A live lock refuses a second computer; a lock untouched for three minutes is abandoned. A running app that finds its lock replaced closes the database and quits.
- A chosen folder uses `journal_mode = DELETE`, `synchronous = FULL` and no mmap; the default per-user folder keeps WAL. A missing recorded folder stops the app with its path instead of falling back to a local copy.
- New `src/main/storage.test.ts` (15 cases): pointer roundtrip and malformed/missing targets, live/dead/stale locks, schema inspection, adopt/move/fresh validation, newer-schema refusal, same-folder refusal, candidate reporting, the copy with its safety copy and sidecar removal, and the failed-copy cleanup. `npm test` is **377 tests across forty-three files**; lint 134 files.
- [ADR 0022](decisions/0022-the-ledger-can-live-in-a-folder-you-choose.md) records the pointer, the lock, the restart and the journaling choice. FEATURES, SECURITY and README describe the feature and its limits.

### Batch 31 — WageRoute Pocket

- **An installable phone view, built from the same money code.** `src/pocket` is a second Vite/React app that builds to static files in `dist-pocket/`. There is no server, account or upload; the manifest and service worker make it installable and it opens with no signal. It reuses `src/shared` and `src/renderer/src/lib/finance.ts` for every calculation, so the phone cannot invent a different Safe to Spend.
- **The phone may only add plain entries.** Expenses and income write new transaction ids; the export appends them to the original tables. The desktop's additive import adds them once. Bill payments and edits to desktop rows are deliberately refused because an additive import cannot carry a claim settlement, an edit or a deletion.
- **First run asks for a file, not an account.** The welcome screen names Settings → Export a backup on the computer. `backup.example.json` opens; a non-backup, a malformed file and a file above 24 MB are refused with a plain reason. A newer schema opens with a warning.

- **Commands and docs.** `npm run pocket:dev`, `pocket:build`, `pocket:preview` and `typecheck:pocket`; the main `typecheck` now runs all three projects. ### Batch 32 — backup restore and claim parents

- **A restore after an erase brings the settings back.** Erase rebuilds a blank profile with the seeded defaults, so an additive merge used to skip every settings key and leave the new ledger on defaults. A merge into a ledger with no history — no account, entry, bill, goal, paycheck or reserve row, tombstones included — now applies the file's values, counting them as added when they differ from the seed; an import into a ledger that already has history still keeps the local values. `backup-merge.test.ts` covers both directions.
- **A merge cannot leave a live claim under a dead parent.** A `bill_reserves` row whose bill was deleted locally (or is absent from the file) is now reported and skipped instead of inserting a live claim against a tombstoned bill. A claim whose *funding cheque* is a tombstone still participates in the replay, because that cheque is what wrote the claim and the money already left; the join in `reconcileBillReserve` no longer filters cheques by `deleted_at`. The bills join still requires a live bill.
- **The claim replay runs inside the merge transaction.** `reconcileAllBillReserves` moved into the import transaction, so a replay failure rolls back the inserted rows instead of committing a half-replayed ledger. A trigger-forced failure test asserts bills, paychecks and reserves all end at zero.
- **A reserve without a `state` is filled, not asked about.** `FieldSpec.hasDefault` lets `findImportProblems` skip a blank schema-defaulted column; a present but invalid state is still one question. A blank state lands `reserved`, so the money is never dropped for want of an answer.

### Batch 50 — both devices derive a bill's set-aside from its payments

- **What the gap was.** ADR 0025 gave the phone the desktop's validation core and mirrored its writers row for row, but one rule stayed behind on the computer: the derivation of whether a claim has been spent. A paycheck's set-aside is a promise, and whether it has been kept follows from the payments in the ledger, so it has to be re-derived rather than remembered — the desktop had been doing that since ADR 0012, inside `reconcileBillReserve`, in SQL next to the `UPDATE` it fed. The phone never ran it. Four concrete divergences followed, all of them the phone reading its own stale state between round trips: tap **Pay** and the payment lands while the claim it settles stays `reserved`, so the bill keeps reading as owed; delete that payment and the claim stays `paid` on the strength of a row that no longer exists; delete the account that held it, same; cut it in half and the claim stays `paid` against a payment that no longer covers it. The round trip hid all four because the desktop's import re-derives everything, which is exactly why a device used *between* round trips — the whole point of a phone — was the one that was wrong.
- **One walk, in a pure module.** `src/shared/claims.ts` exports `deriveClaimStates(claims, payments)`, returning each claim's `state`, the `settledBy` payment and a `changed` flag. Plain facts in, plain answers out — no SQLite, no Electron, no DOM, no clock, the same shape as `writeRules.ts` and `deletion.ts` and for the same reason: a rule two devices share cannot live where either device's storage reaches. The ordering moved inside the rule too (claims by cycle, then `created_at`, then `id`; payments by `occurred_on`, then `created_at`, then `id`, compared by code unit, which is SQLite's `BINARY` collation), because *which claim a payment settles first* is a money decision that must not depend on which caller remembered to write an `ORDER BY`. Both desktop `ORDER BY` clauses were deleted with it.
- **The desktop keeps its SQL and loses its arithmetic.** `reconcileBillReserve` in `src/main/db/repo/bills.ts` is now two reads, one call, and an `UPDATE` per changed claim — 96 lines to 43. Its thirty reserve tests pass unchanged, which is the proof that this was an extraction and not a redefinition.
- **The phone mirrors the call sites, not the SQL.** `reconcileBillClaims(tables, billId)` runs after an entry that pays a bill (the two kinds that pay; an adjustment may name a bill without paying one), after an edit that moves amount, kind or date, after `deleteEntries` for every bill a doomed row was paying, and after `deleteAccount` over the same expanded set. `reconcileAllBillClaims` is the import backstop in `store.ts`, run after both a first file and a merge, because a payment typed here after the computer wrote its export is not in that file's arithmetic. A claim whose cheque row is missing is skipped rather than guessed open, mirroring the desktop's `INNER JOIN`.
- **A claim moves only when the derivation disagrees.** The `changed` flag gates the write on both devices. Without it every entry write would bump every claim's `revision` on both copies, and the phone would win merges it had nothing to contribute to; with it, an untouched claim keeps the computer's copy exactly. Parity is therefore asserted about *state*, not about ids — each device mints its own payment id, so `settled_by` can never agree across them.
- **Verified in the running app, not only in tests.** Against the built shell on port 4178 (`dist-pocket/assets/index-xmkDXpGt.js` — the hash served, so the artifact driven is the artifact built) opened on a real desktop ledger exported by the app's own backup builder, holding one $1,200 bill whose claim was stored `reserved`: the phone's bill list read **Due**, `Mark paid` wrote the payment and moved the claim to `paid` at revision 2 with `settled_by` naming this phone's own row; deleting that payment reopened it (`reserved`, revision 3, `settled_by` null); paying it again reached revision 4. `IndexedDB` was read back at every step for ground truth and no console message appeared at any point.
- **That pass caught a silent write loss next door.** While testing the edit path — cut the payment to $60, the claim should reopen — the sheet closed, nothing errored, and `IndexedDB` showed the row exactly as it had been. The same edit reproduced in Node against the same tables, so it was the writer, not the UI. `patchEntry` patched the incoming copy, called `resolveEntryCategory`, and then took *the resolved copy* as the result; that copy is derived from the pre-patch tables, so every edit arriving with a category was thrown away whole. The phone's entry sheet sends a category with every save, so on this device **every** entry edit was a no-op that reported success. The transfer branch of the same function already had the right shape (`const base = resolved ? resolved.tables : tables`), so the fix adopts it: resolve first, patch on the copy that carries the new row (`src/pocket/lib/write.ts:1178`). Rebuilt and driven again on the same shell, the edit then wrote: the payment went to −$60.00 at revision 2 and the claim reopened to `reserved` at revision 5 with `settled_by` cleared, and putting it back to $1,200.00 settled the claim again at revision 6. Before the fix the identical keystrokes closed the sheet, raised no error, and left both rows untouched.
- **A cold review of this batch found its mirror image, and it was worse.** The same pass that read the new module backwards asked what the phone does with a fact the file never carried, and the answer was: it invents one. `reconcileBillClaims` read a claim's due day with `integer(row, 'due_day')`, and the row readers in `src/pocket/lib/rows.ts` answer a missing key with `0`. A due day of `0` is not an error to the walk — it is a day, and it makes the cheque's day-of-month *always* greater than it, which opens the carry gate for every claim. So a payment dated in November settled last month's claim on the phone while the computer held it reserved. This is not a hypothetical file: released v1.2.0 stops at schema 8, where `bill_reserves` has no `due_day` column, so **every backup the shipped desktop writes** has claims with no due day, and a backup file is the only ledger a phone ever holds. The review's scenario was reproduced first (one $1,200 monthly bill due on the 1st, an October claim written by a 1 October cheque and a November claim written by a 5 November cheque, one payment on 2 November: desktop `reserved`/`reserved`, phone `paid`/`reserved`), then fixed.
- **The fix is the desktop's own rule, not a new one.** `mergeBackup` has filled that key for years: a claim row arriving with no `due_day` takes the bill's own due day (`src/main/db/backup.ts:562`), which is what the v9 migration backfilled the column from. The phone now reads a claim's facts only where the computer's schema would have held the row — cycle present and seven characters, the cheque's date and the row's stamp present, due day resolved from the row then the bill and required to be a real day — and leaves any claim it cannot read exactly as the file has it, which is what `src/shared/claims.ts` has always promised in its docstring and nothing enforced. Rows sharing one id now collapse to the one the merge keeps, by the shared `incomingWins` comparator rather than a new tie-break, so a hand-edited file cannot make one promise collect money twice. Both fixes live at the phone's boundary, where JSON arrives; the desktop reads the same facts out of columns its `CHECK` constraints already vouch for, so it needed no guard.
- **Two findings the review raised and verification closed without a change.** A zero-amount claim reads as covered by no money at all — but the computer's read already filters `amount_cents > 0` and the phone's filter says the same, so neither device can hand one over; the precondition is now written where it can be checked. And a claim is not re-derived when a bill's frequency or due day is edited — on *either* device, deliberately: a claim keeps the day and strategy it was written with, which is what the carry rule in ADR 0012 depends on, so the phone was level with the computer here rather than behind it. ADR 0028 now states both.
- **The Bills footnote corrected the same day.** Its fine print promised the phone's payment *settles its reservation the next time you import on the desktop* — true when written, untrue after this batch, and the kind of sentence a person relies on. It now says the reservation settles on this phone, the way the desktop does (`src/pocket/screens/Bills.tsx:465`).
- **Tests.** `src/shared/claims.test.ts` is new with nine cases over the pure walk (a payment that covers a claim settles it and names itself, a part payment that holds it open until the row filling the gap settles it, a refund reversing the most recent coverage, the cheque-that-has-not-arrived gate, the due-day carry, quarterly smoothing, an over-payment that cannot spill into the next claim, order independence plus an empty ledger, and a claim that already agrees reporting `changed: false`). `src/pocket/lib/claims-parity.test.ts` is new with seven: the same money fact driven through the real desktop repository and the real phone writer over a ledger exported from the first, agreeing on state and revision while each names its own payment, the end-to-end phone write → backup → `mergeBackup` → `paid` case, and three driven by a cold review of exactly this batch — a backup in the shape the **released** desktop writes (v1.2.0 stops at schema 8, where a claim row has no `due_day` key at all, so this is every file a phone will ever be handed) holds a carried claim open when the cheque arrived before the due day, settles it when it arrived after, and a file repeating one claim id cannot let a half payment close a whole claim. `src/pocket/lib/write.test.ts` went from 22 to 30, covering pay, delete-reopens, part payment, cut-back-reopens, account delete, the import replay, an idempotent reconcile leaving every row byte-identical, and the regression that an edit carrying its category writes the row it was shown. `npm test` is **572 tests across fifty-seven files**; lint is 180 files clean; all three type checks, `npm run build`, `npm run pocket:build` and `npx electron-builder --dir --publish never` pass.

### Batch 52 — both halves of the app in a container, and one command for them

- **What was asked for was a `docker run`.** The honest answer needed splitting first: Pocket is a folder of static files, so a container can genuinely serve it to a port, and it does that today only if someone finds a host. The desktop image therefore runs the real Linux Electron build against Xvfb and streams that window out — x11vnc on loopback inside the container, noVNC on 6080 — which is a desktop watched through a websocket, not an app served to users. That distinction is written at the top of the section it belongs to rather than left to the reader.
- **The Pocket image was measured, not assumed.** 77.3 MB, nginx plus the files `npm run pocket:build` writes, no Node or toolchain in the runtime layer. ### Batch 53 — encryption the owner chooses, on the disk, in the export, and on the phone

- **The brief was an option, not a default.** *"add encryption that will work… remember that it isn't a default, it's an option… give users an option + an option to change to and from in the future."* That sentence decided the shape of everything below: a card in Settings that starts off, a change of mind that costs one click in each direction, and an architecture where custody is re-wrappable so "change it later" is never a data-loss event. [ADR 0029](decisions/0029-encryption-is-a-choice-on-the-disk-and-on-the-way-out.md) records the choice and supersedes [ADR 0019](decisions/0019-the-ledger-is-plaintext-at-rest.md); [ADR 0020](decisions/0020-there-is-no-app-lock.md) was amended to say why this is encryption of a file and not the screen lock it still refuses to be.
- **A spike first, because the whole plan rests on one reversible file.** Phase 0 drove plain → encrypted → plain on a scratch ledger before any UI existed: the pages change, the row counts and amounts survive, and the way back really is a rewrite rather than a flag. That ordering was the gate for committing to anything else.
- **The driver is a fork, and that is the widest part of the change.** `better-sqlite3` is replaced by `better-sqlite3-multiple-ciphers` ^13.0.3 — the only database module in `package.json`, imported at `src/main/db/connection.ts:1`. Nothing was vendored and the codec is pinned to `chacha20` before any other pragma. The cost is honest: a fork is a bigger supply-chain surface than the upstream module it replaces, the shipped binary is the fork's own prebuild rather than something this project compiles (so what `npm ci` guarantees is the *file*, pinned by the lockfile, not a build of it), and a build that swaps the driver back can no longer open a keyed ledger at all (`SQLITE_NOTADB`) — which makes an encrypted file a **downgrade trap**, recorded in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md#known-weaknesses) rather than hidden.
- **Custody is a choice with three answers and a table that cannot be skipped.** `ledger.key.json` (`0600`) holds only **wraps** of a 32-byte data key: a `safeStorage` device wrap, a passphrase wrap (scrypt `N=32768, r=8, p=1` then AES-256-GCM), or both with either one sufficient. The honesty table — *what it protects against / what it does not stop / if you lose it* — lives in `src/shared/encryption.ts` as part of the type, so an option without all three fails the build, and `EncryptionCard.tsx` renders those columns instead of inventing its own reassurance. `deviceCustodyAvailable()` turns a headless keychain throw into *this machine offers no keychain*, so the option is withheld with its reason rather than failing at the next launch. Re-wrapping is the reversible half: add-device, remove-device, add-passphrase, change-passphrase, remove-passphrase — five actions, no page rewritten, so the custody decision can be revised as often as the owner likes.
- **A locked ledger has no application behind it.** Boot opens the ledger before registering IPC (`src/main/index.ts:337` → `:341`), so the locked state has no ledger window and no ledger channel to ask a question of — only `?mode=unlock` and its passphrase field. The unlock channels are deliberately **outside** `IpcContract`, because a contract the boot gate cannot answer is a contract that lies. A ciphertext file whose marker is missing is refused, not guessed at; a refusal logs `ledger-locked`, `ledger-no-custody` or `key-file` and never the phrase, because an app that is not holding the key cannot count guesses either.
- **Both directions rewrite the file, so the transition is built like a transaction.** A `VACUUM INTO` pre-copy first, a `pending` marker written before the pragma flip, `secure_delete` with `locking_mode=EXCLUSIVE` and `journal_mode=delete` for the rewrite, verification on a fresh connection including an `integrity_check`, the journal mode restored, the safety copy deleted only once the change has proved itself, and `reconcilePending()` reading the marker at the next launch to put things back. Turning it off takes the same care in the other direction and removes the key file as the last step — because a keyed copy whose marker has just been deleted is unreadable by construction, including by the person who asked.
- **The app's own copies follow the ledger.** Every dated copy — `daily-*`, `pre-upgrade-*`, `pre-erase-*` — is taken with `VACUUM INTO` and probed with the same data key (`src/main/storage.ts:383-406`); `sqlite3`'s backup API was replaced precisely because it refuses a codec'd database, so a keyed ledger would otherwise stop backing itself up in silence. A data-folder move carries `ledger.key.json` with it and the test reads 1,234 cents back out of the new folder after unlocking there (`src/main/storage.test.ts:318-341`), which is also what makes the move undoable.
- **Phase 3: an export can be sealed, and both devices read the same envelope.** One shared contract (`src/shared/envelope.ts`) — `wageroute-envelope` v1, AES-256-GCM, PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 at 600 000 rounds with accepted rails 100 000–2 000 000, 16-byte salt, 12-byte IV, 16-byte tag, `exportedAt` the only field in the clear, failures typed `shape | passphrase`. The desktop reads and writes it with `node:crypto`, the phone with WebCrypto, and `src/shared/envelope.test.ts` pins the format from both sides so neither can drift. The UI change is small and the honesty is the point: *Export full backup* now asks **plain or sealed**, the sealed choice says in one sentence what it does and who can still test guesses, and the import wizard stops at a passphrase step and shows **no rows** until the file opens.
- **Phase 4: the phone keeps a vault, and the browser decides whether it can.** `wageroute-vault` v1 marks one sealed record in IndexedDB; the session key is non-extractable and module-scope, so reloading the page really locks the copy until the phrase is typed. Sealed and plain states are one union under the same key, and `pendingSeal` carries a file that arrived sealed but has not been opened.
- **The constraint that nearly got missed: a cipher is a privilege of the origin.** `crypto.subtle` exists only in a secure context, so Pocket over a plain-HTTP LAN address has **no cipher at all** — not a slow one, not a weaker one. `src/pocket/lib/webcrypto.ts:44` names that state `NO_CIPHER_HERE`, `hasCipher()` asks for it, and every seal, lock and open path is disabled instead of half-working; the destructive *Forget this phone's copy* is refused too, so a page that cannot prove it holds a key never offers to destroy one. `src/pocket/lib/without-cipher.ts` exists so tests can drive that page rather than argue about it.
- **What was proved, and how.** 723 tests across seventy files, of which fifteen suites are this layer: the custody table and the passphrase rule, the envelope contract and the node reader, key wraps, the transition and its rollback, the device round trip, the unlock gate, a sealed file written and read back, the unlock window, five phone suites, plus the export modal and the wizard's passphrase step. The transition ran end to end **inside the running app** on scratch ledgers in `/private/tmp` — never against the live ledger, and never in a package.
- **What is not proved, and must not be promised.** A packaged build has **never** opened a keyed ledger, run the transition, or restored from a keyed `VACUUM INTO` copy; that is the shape users actually run, so the release checklist below carries it as an open item. No real handset has run any of this — the vault screens are driven in jsdom and the WebCrypto half was measured in a laptop Chromium — so **no document may quote an unlock delay**, and the phone-CPU cost of 600 000 PBKDF2 rounds is unmeasured, as is whether iOS Safari evicts the IndexedDB copy under storage pressure. The published `1.2.0` has never been pointed at an encrypted file by us (it is a plain-SQLITE `SQLITE_NOTADB` path by the driver's own contract). A plain-HTTP page's refusal has been tested by faking the absence of `crypto.subtle`, not by a browser that really lacks it. Nothing here is a claim about what an attacker with the file cannot do in a year.
- **Docs.** [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md#encryption-at-rest-and-key-custody) gained the layer's own section — driver, custody, the locked-boot consequence, the reversible transition, sealed exports and the phone vault — and its data-at-rest table, threat model, known-weakness list, hardening ladder and incident-response procedures were rewritten against it; the four rows that used to read "no encryption at rest" now name what is opt-in, what is unreleased and what is unproven. The rest of the sweep — `README.md`, `CHANGELOG.md`, `ARCHITECTURE.md`, `DATA-MODEL.md`, `DEPLOYMENT.md`, `FEATURES.md`, `IMPORT-EXPORT.md`, `POCKET.md`, `RELEASING.md`, `DEVELOPMENT.md` and the two docker files' volume comments — is the work recorded in the progress file alongside this entry.

### Batch 55 — the edge shape ADR 0030 owed: TLS and a login outside the app

- **What this batch is.** [ADR 0030](decisions/0030-self-hosting-puts-identity-at-the-edge.md) decided that a certificate and a login belong at the edge and that the repo's obligation was **a worked example, not an authentication implementation**. Until now the example was owed and the docs only named the shape in a sentence. This batch pays that debt: `docker/edge.Caddyfile` and an `edge` profile in `docker-compose.yml`, plus the chapter in [DEPLOYMENT.md](DEPLOYMENT.md#tls-a-login-and-more-than-one-person) that says how the whole thing fails closed. No app file changed — the point of the decision is that there is nothing in the app to change.
- **The default shape is untouched, and that was the first requirement.** Every new service sits behind `profiles: [edge]`, so `docker compose up --build` still starts exactly `pocket` and `desktop` on `127.0.0.1`; `docker compose config --services` with no environment prints those two and nothing else. The edge adds `caddy` (image pinned to `caddy:2.11.7-alpine`, because a proxy that decides who may read a ledger is not something to take on a floating tag), `pocket-ann`, `desktop-ann`, and two volumes: `wageroute-ann-data` (one tenant, one volume — the whole multi-user mechanism) and `wageroute-caddy`, which exists so Caddy's local CA survives the container being recreated.
- **Two compose/Caddy facts had to be discovered by being wrong.** Caddy 2.11.7 rejects `forward_auth` outright unless the block carries the `uri` subdirective, so the first draft (`forward_auth <addr>` plus a bare path) failed validation and was restructured. And compose's `${VAR:?error}` — the obvious way to refuse a half-configured edge — is evaluated while parsing the **whole file**: the check that should have guarded only the profiled services made `docker compose config --services` fail for `desktop` and `pocket` too, i.e. it would have broken the default command for every person who never turns this on. The values are therefore passed as `${VAR:-}` and refused **inside** the container, in an entrypoint guard that names the missing variable and exits 1 before Caddy starts. Proven both ways: the guard printed `edge: WAGEROUTE_DOMAIN is not set — see docker/edge.Caddyfile` and exited 1; with all three set, Caddy came up.
- **What "fail closed" means here, item by item.** `WAGEROUTE_BIND` defaults to `127.0.0.1`, so an edge configured halfway is a proxy nobody can reach rather than a ledger everybody can; the tenant containers have **no `ports:` at all**, so there is no container address to walk past the login with; `forward_auth` proxies nothing unless the provider answers `2xx`; nothing reads an identity header to pick a folder — the *site block* picks the upstream, and `copy_headers` is deliberately absent because the app has no code to read an identity with. The file also records the two shapes ADR 0030 supports but does not implement for you: TLS with the `forward_auth` blocks deleted is HTTPS with no door, and a second person is a copy of both blocks plus their own volume.
- **Driven end to end on 2026-11-04 against images built from this tree.** `caddy validate` reports *Valid configuration* with zero warnings; through the proxy, `https://pocket.ann.test/` answered **401 with the auth stub's own body** (nothing proxied) and **200 with a session cookie**, and the 200 carried `sw.js` (3492 bytes), the hashed bundle and `content-security-policy: frame-ancestors 'none'`; `https://desktop.ann.test/vnc.html` answered the same way (15212 bytes); TLS verified against the root extracted from the `wageroute-caddy` volume. The Pocket image was rebuilt from the current tree and its served bundle contains `wageroute-vault`, `wageroute-envelope` and the no-cipher message, so the tested artifact is this repository's code, not a published build. Re-checked on the final tree: the documented `docker run … caddy validate` one-liner prints *Valid configuration*, `docker compose cp caddy:/data/caddy/pki/authorities/local/root.crt` yields the 627-byte root (`CN=Caddy Local Authority - 2026 ECC Root`), and both compose renders (default with no environment, edge with it, `host_ip: 192.168.1.20` on 443) come out as the docs say.
- **A defect found while writing the teardown line.** `docker compose down -v` **skips services behind `profiles:`**, so the command that looks like closing the edge leaves the proxy listening — it reported the network "still in use" and left Caddy up while I checked it. `COMPOSE_PROFILES=edge docker compose down` is what actually stops it. That is now in the fail-closed list and in [DEVELOPMENT.md](DEVELOPMENT.md), because a doc that tells someone how to open a door and not how to shut it is the worse kind of example.

A blank window was therefore blank forever — no retry, no log line, nothing for *Copy diagnostics* — and [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md)'s "a boot failure is visible… it cannot end up running with no window" was true in letter, because there *was* a window, and false in substance.
- **The module.** `src/main/window-load.ts` takes the two events as listeners and three actions as functions and owns one decision: `did-finish-load` ends the watch; `did-fail-load` on the main frame, or **12 seconds of neither** (`LOAD_TIMEOUT_MS`), is a problem; the first two problems reload the document and the third says so once and never again. Silence counts because it is the only signal an empty initial document gives. A sub-resource failure does not — a missing icon is not a blank screen, and reloading would throw away the page the user is looking at. Neither does `ERR_ABORTED` (-3), which is what this module's own retry does to the load still in flight: without that rule a single slow-but-live page can abort itself twice and end in a report about a screen that was on its way. Nothing in the module imports Electron and the clock is a parameter, which is the only reason the retry limit is a tested number rather than a guess.
- **Six cases in `src/main/window-load.test.ts`**, all against an injected queue of timers: a screen that arrives ends the watch; silence is retried twice and then reported once, with no reload after the report; `did-fail-load` is retried without waiting for the clock; a sub-resource failure changes nothing and leaves the clock armed; an abort caused by the retry itself spends no retry and leaves the clock armed; a load that recovers after a retry never reports and leaves no timer running. Plus the pure matrix over `nextLoadAction` and the two constants the shipped watch uses. The file is in the package.json test list (seventy-three), which `scripts/check-test-list.mjs` enforces.
- **The two wires, and why they end differently.** `src/main/index.ts:124` watches the main window: two `[window-load]` notes, then `logCrash('window-load', …)`, a dialog that says the ledger was not changed and points at the log file, and `app.quit()` — a blank main window is the app's only surface, so stopping is the honest end. `src/main/unlock-prompt.ts:118` watches the prompt: the same two notes under `[unlock-prompt-load]`, then `logCrash` and `dispose()` — and closing the prompt is what makes the launch's passphrase wait resolve as *no answer*, so the existing `ledger-locked` refusal takes over. The watchdog opens no ledger and touches no key file on either path, and each `load()` closure returns at once if its window is gone, because a watch can outlive the window it was armed for.
- **Driven, not asserted.** Against the built main process on scratch profiles under `/private/tmp`, with `ELECTRON_RENDERER_URL` pointed at a port nothing listens on: a plain profile wrote `document load retry 1 of 2: load failed: ERR_CONNECTION_REFUSED (-102)`, `retry 2 of 2`, then `[window-load] Error: WageRoute opened a window and its screen never arrived: load failed: ERR_CONNECTION_REFUSED (-102) after 2 retries`, and the process was gone inside the 22 s the driver waited. A **byte copy of a keyed ledger** put the same dead URL in front of the unlock window: two `[unlock-prompt-load]` notes, `Error: The passphrase prompt never appeared: … after 2 retries`, then the pre-existing `[ledger-locked] LedgerLockedError` handover, with that scratch ledger and its key file unchanged. A third drive used a server that answers the first request 15 s late and the second in 500 ms: one line in the log — `document load retry 1 of 2: no screen after 12000ms` — the server saw the replacement request 16 ms after it, the app was alive at 25 s and nothing was reported. A fourth, with the same server answering both requests late, produced notes at +12.0 s and +24.0 s and no report: the retry re-issues the load, and the abort that causes spent no retry of its own. What no drive covered is the dialog's on-screen half, which needs a display this session cannot capture.
- **The cost, stated.** A load that fails loudly is retried back to back and reported in under a second (the three `-102` lines above sit 9 ms and 8 ms apart). The silence case costs 12 s, a reload, 12 s, another reload, and the message at ~36 s. Before this batch that wait was infinite and silent. 12 s is comfortably longer than every document load observed this session (the slowest committed in 390 ms) and is not tuned against anything else — the number is a first estimate, and the retry limit of 2 is the part that has a reason: reloading is the only remedy the real defect was ever seen to respond to.

[ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md) gained the watch in the boot sequence, the two new scopes in the log section (a note per retry, one crash when the retries are spent), and `src/main/window-load.ts` in the module map. ### Batch 60 — a packaged drive that did not stay in its own folder, and the rule that came out of it

- **What was attempted.** The five open checklist items that only a packaged app can answer (the cross-month wall reads, the two-cheque-then-third case, the due-day edit, the allocator preview priority, and the external-link guard). - **The same boot also moved the live ledger to schema 10, and that is why #163 is no longer optional.** The file is now `user_version` 10 while `/Applications/WageRoute.app` is the published **1.2.0**, whose schema ceiling is 8; the installed app refuses it before it renders anything.
