# 0028 — Both devices derive a bill's set-aside from its payments

**Status:** Accepted (2026-11-03)

## Context

[ADR 0025](0025-the-phone-writes-through-the-desktops-own-rules.md) gave the phone the desktop's validation core and mirrored every writer row-for-row. One thing stayed behind on the computer: the derivation of a claim's state.

A paycheck that sets aside $120 for rent writes a `bill_reserves` row — a *claim* — and the wall withholds that money until the rent is actually paid. Whether it has been is not a fact either device can remember; it follows from the payments the ledger holds. The desktop had been deriving it since [ADR 0012](0012-the-wall-remembers-its-claims.md) and had been hardening it through Batches 23, 24 and 26 (partial payments, refunds, cheque carry, quarterly smoothing). That derivation lived inside `reconcileBillReserve`, in SQL, next to the `UPDATE` it feeds.

So the phone never ran it. `src/pocket/lib/write.ts` touched `bill_reserves` in exactly two places — `deleteBill` and its deletion summary — while `src/pocket/lib/compute.ts` read the **stored** state to decide what the wall still owes. Four concrete divergences followed:

- Tap **Pay** on the phone's own bills screen (`Bills.tsx:322` → `payBill`) and the payment lands in the ledger, but the claim it settles stays `reserved` on that device. The bill keeps reading as money still owed.
- Delete that payment and the claim stays `paid` on the strength of a row that no longer exists.
- Delete the account that held the payment: same.
- Cut the payment in half: the claim stays `paid` against a payment that no longer covers it.

The round trip hid it. The desktop's import calls `reconcileAllBillReserves` inside the merge transaction (`src/main/db/backup.ts:644`), so any file the computer writes is already correct, and the phone's next import inherits that. A device that is used between two round trips — which is the entire point of a phone — is the one reading its own stale state. Batch 40 had softened two of these paths: the Pay prompt subtracts what the month has already paid (`billPaidThisMonthCents`, `src/pocket/lib/write.ts:288`) instead of trusting the claim, and a bill with *no* current-cycle claim reads as paid once its payments cover it (`src/pocket/lib/compute.ts:116`). Neither helps the case above — a stored `reserved` claim outranks the payments on the wall, which is exactly why the bill keeps reading as owed after the phone pays it.

## Decision

**One walk, in a pure module, run by both devices at the desktop's own call sites.**

1. **`src/shared/claims.ts` owns the derivation.** `deriveClaimStates(claims, payments)` takes plain fact objects and returns each claim's `state`, the `settledBy` payment and a `changed` flag. No SQLite, no Electron, no DOM, no clock: the same shape as `writeRules.ts` and `deletion.ts`, for the same reason — a rule two devices share has to live where neither device's storage reaches.
2. **The ordering belongs to the rule, not to the query.** Claims are sorted by `cycle`, then `created_at`, then `id`; payments by `occurred_on`, then `created_at`, then `id`, *inside* the module, using code-unit comparison (SQLite's `BINARY` collation). Which claim a payment settles first is a money decision; it cannot be left to whichever caller wrote the `ORDER BY`. Both SQL `ORDER BY` clauses were deleted with it.
3. **The desktop keeps its SQL and loses its arithmetic.** `reconcileBillReserve` is now two reads, one call and an `UPDATE` per changed claim. Its thirty reserve tests pass unchanged, which is the point: this is an extraction, not a redefinition.
4. **The phone mirrors the desktop's call sites, not its SQL.** `reconcileBillClaims(tables, billId)` is called after an entry that pays a bill (the two kinds that pay, not an adjustment that merely names one), after an entry edit that moves amount, kind or date, after `deleteEntries` for every bill a doomed row was paying, and after `deleteAccount` over the same expanded set. `reconcileAllBillClaims` is the phone's import backstop, run in `store.ts` after both a first file and a merge, because a payment typed after the export is not in the file's arithmetic.
5. **A claim moves only when the derivation disagrees with what is stored.** `changed` is the guard on both devices. Without it, every write would bump every claim's `revision` on both copies, and the phone's revision inflation would win merges it had nothing to contribute to. An untouched claim keeps the computer's copy.
6. **Parity is asserted about state, not about ids.** `settled_by` names the payment that closed the claim, and each device mints its own payment id, so the two can never agree on that column. `claims-parity.test.ts` compares state and `revision`, and checks each side names *its own* payment.
7. **The phone reads a fact only where the desktop's schema would have guaranteed it.** A backup row carries no constraints, and the row readers answer a missing key with `0` or `''` — values that look in-domain to the walk while saying something the computer never meant. So `reconcileBillClaims` hands the walk a claim only when its cycle, dates and cheque are there to be read, and takes the due day from the row, from the bill, or not at all. The middle case is not a corner: released v1.2.0 stops at schema 8, where a `bill_reserves` row has no `due_day` key, so *every file the shipped desktop writes* arrives without one, and a coerced `0` opens the due-day gate and settles a claim the computer holds reserved. The bill's own due day is what the same row gets on the computer — `mergeBackup` fills it in exactly that way (`src/main/db/backup.ts:562`), which is where the v9 migration backfilled the column from. Two rows under one id collapse to the one the merge keeps, so a hand-edited file cannot make one promise collect money twice.

## Consequences

- Paying, unpaying, shrinking or moving a bill payment now settles and reopens the claim on the phone in the same keystroke it does on the computer. The phone's wall is right between round trips instead of after them.
- A phone payment reaching the desktop needs no special case: it is a `transactions` row like any other, and the import's own backstop settles the claim (pinned end-to-end, phone write → backup → `mergeBackup` → claim `paid`).
- The rule now has one home. A future change to carry, smoothing or refund handling lands in `src/shared/claims.ts` and is immediately true on both devices; a change made in the desktop's repository again would be caught by `claims-parity.test.ts`.
- The `changed` guard means a claim a device cannot check is left exactly as the other device wrote it. A phone holding a claim whose cheque row is missing skips it, the same way the desktop's `INNER JOIN` does, rather than guessing it open.
- Deriving on both devices did not make the stored snapshot meaningless, and it is not re-derived when a bill is edited. A claim keeps the `due_day` and `strategy` it was written with — that is what the carry rule in [ADR 0012](0012-the-wall-remembers-its-claims.md) depends on, so switching a bill to `all_checks` mid-month cannot turn a month already carried into a second instalment. Changing a bill's frequency or due day therefore leaves its existing claims alone on *both* devices until the next payment or import replays them; the phone is not behind the computer here, it is level with it.
- Two things this does not fix, stated rather than implied: the phone's monthly payment figure (`billPaidThisMonthCents`) is a separate display rule that reads only the current month, and a refund can only reach the phone through an import. Neither form can post one: the create path signs a bill payment as money leaving (`normalizeSignedCents`, `src/shared/kinds.ts:35`) and the edit path answers a contradicted sign with a refusal (`signedAmountError`), so the refund walk is exercised by the merge backstop and by `src/shared/claims.test.ts`, not by a keystroke.
